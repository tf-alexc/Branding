## Brand Architecture: Four Pillars

TrustFlight operates as a single platform with four capability pillars — not a holding company, not a group of companies.

| Pillar | Capability Brand | Positioning | Key Stats |
|--------|-----------------|-------------|-----------|
| Technology | TrustFlight (direct) | AI-native Agentic Platform, SaaS safety/security software | 1,600+ organisations, 120 countries |
| Training & Expertise | Baines Simmons | "Building the competence behind aerospace and defence safety" | 25 years, 750+ orgs, 40+ regulators advised, 200,000+ trained |
| Security | Redline Assured Security | "Government-standard security for aviation and critical infrastructure" | 20+ years, ICAO appointed UK Aviation Security Training Centre, 200+ clients |
| Crisis Response | Kenyon International | "120 years of being there when it matters most" | 2,500+ specialist responders, 500+ organisations |

**How to refer to sub-brands:** They are "capabilities" of TrustFlight. Never "subsidiaries," "divisions," "business units," or "sister companies."

## Brand Voice & Tone

**Confident, not arrogant** — State value clearly. No superlatives without evidence. Let scale and heritage speak.

**Technical, not jargon-heavy** — Use industry terms (SMS, QMS, CAMO, AOC, SeMS) but define acronyms on first use in external content.

**Human, not corporate** — Direct, active language. If it makes you wince when read aloud, rewrite it.

| Say This | Not This |
|----------|----------|
| "We help you meet EASA requirements" | "We enable compliance optimization" |
| "Your team will know exactly what to do" | "We deliver actionable insights that drive operational excellence" |
| "We connect your safety data to your training records" | "We leverage synergies across the safety ecosystem" |
| "This saves you time" | "This unlocks efficiency gains" |
| "We built this for aerospace" | "Our best-in-class solution delivers unparalleled value" |

**Connected, not siloed** — Never fragment. When one capability is mentioned, connect it to the others. Never tell a customer to "reach out to our security team" or "contact Baines Simmons" — make the connection yourself.

| Say This | Not This |
|----------|----------|
| "I'll connect you with our security capability and we'll coordinate the assessment together" | "You should reach out to Redline for security support" |
| "Let me bring in our training team to design a programme around the gaps in your audit" | "Contact Baines Simmons directly for training" |

## Writing Standards

**Naming rules:**
- TrustFlight = one word, capital T, capital F. Always.
- Never "TrustFlight Group" — just TrustFlight
- Never "a TrustFlight company" as an endorsement line
- Never "TrustFlight Services" — retired, use Baines Simmons
- Sub-brands = "capabilities" (never subsidiary, division, business unit)
- "crisis management and response" — not "emergency management and response"
- Kenyon: use "exercises" — not "drills" (approved terminology)

**Style rules:**
- Default to British English for UK, EMEA, APAC; American English for North America
- Never use em dashes — use hyphens, colons, commas, or restructure the sentence
- All body text left-aligned, never justified
- Active voice. Short sentences. Clarity first.
- Safety and security are distinct but connected disciplines — never present them as the same thing

**Do:**
- Lead with TrustFlight at the corporate level, then zoom into the relevant capability
- Frame Baines Simmons, Redline, and Kenyon as capabilities of TrustFlight
- Use "Aerospace Safety Intelligence Platform" as the primary descriptor in every corporate context
- Position Smart Suite as the future and AI innovation engine
- Reference the four pillars by name: Technology, Training & Expertise, Security, Crisis Response
- Celebrate heritage: Redline 20 years, Baines Simmons 25 years, Kenyon 120 years

**Don't:**
- Don't use "TrustFlight Services," "TrustFlight Group," or "a TrustFlight company"
- Don't describe TrustFlight as "a software company"
- Don't lead with sub-brands when the audience is C-suite or enterprise-level
- Don't present safety and security as the same discipline
- Don't position TrustFlight as multi-industry — we own aerospace
- Don't reference private equity, acquisitions, or corporate transactions in customer-facing materials
